Tell Me About Your Summer! — A summer story in four scenes

AUTHORED OFFLINE TEACHER PACK · NO LIVE AI
All material is included in this file. Independent English/PE review and classroom evaluation are pending. Private role cards and answer keys are for teacher-controlled distribution.

English: Write a coherent 50–70 word fictional summer narrative with four ordered events, a turning point and a suitable ending.
Physical: Create four comfortable, stable poses or hand-tableaux; change together on a spoken cue and hold each agreed pose for two counts while respecting personal space.
Layout: Groups of four use four numbered paper spots or four desk-card positions. Learners choose comfortable standing, seated or hand-tableau versions. No lifting, balancing on equipment or physical contact. Rotate narrator, pose designer, sequence checker and writer.

All stages

1. Hear the cue, hold the scene

5 minutes · TPR warm-up · Pairs

Say this

Listen to the story cue. Make a comfortable pose or hand picture. Hold for two counts, then relax. Change together when your narrator speaks.

Procedure

  1. 0–1 min · The teacher narrates WALK, LOOK AT A MAP, LOOK SURPRISED and ASK FOR HELP while modelling four comfortable poses or hand gestures.
  2. 1–3 min · Learners respond to the spoken cue, hold the pose for two counts, then relax. Agree a small comfortable movement boundary.
  3. 3–5 min · A learner gives a cue while partners change together. Check controlled transitions, not acting skill.

Teacher role

  • Model the supplied language or action, then check understanding with one learner.
  • Observe the English and physical criteria separately; record support and use the agreed access version.

Learner role

  • Attend to the model and use the supplied language or picture cards.
  • Take a meaningful turn, check the outcome and help partners clarify uncertainty.

2. Discover the model

8 minutes · Model & notice · Pairs

Say this

Read the model. What is the situation? Now answer: What is the turning point, and how is the problem solved? Compare with a partner before checking the answer.

Procedure

  1. 0–3 min · Read the model once for the situation, then identify useful language.
  2. 3–6 min · Answer: What is the turning point, and how is the problem solved?
  3. 6–8 min · Compare answers with the teacher key: The map blows away; they ask a park helper and find the gate.

3. Sequence the turning point

8 minutes · Guided practice · Pairs

Say this

Read the model story. Find the opening, problem and ending. Put the four event strips in order. Say the sequence phrases and rehearse four comfortable poses or hand pictures.

Procedure

  1. 0–3 min · Read the original narrative. Find the opening, the map problem and the resolution; mark the four sequence phrases.
  2. 3–6 min · Pairs arrange event strips: walk to park; look at map; map blows away; ask helper and return home. Explain why the problem precedes the solution.
  3. 6–8 min · Rehearse four poses or hand-tableaux in that order. The narrator says one sequence phrase at each transition.

4. A summer story in four scenes

17 minutes · Communicative movement task · Groups

Say this

A and B, keep your cards private. Tell each other the missing events in the new beach story. Agree four scenes. Make four comfortable poses or hand pictures as your narrator speaks. Now write your own 50–70 word story. Check the problem and ending with a partner, then improve one sentence.

Procedure

  1. 0–3 min · Give A and B their private event cards. Exchange the missing information orally and agree four scenes without showing the cards.
  2. 3–13 min · Create and perform four comfortable poses or hand-tableaux as the narrator says the sequence phrases. The checker verifies the turning point and resolution.
  3. 13–17 min · Each learner writes a 50–70 word version. Partners check the narrative against the writing sheet, revise one unclear link and rotate the physical roles.

Teacher role

  • Distribute the private roles and demonstrate the information-gap rule without revealing the answer.
  • Observe the English and physical criteria separately; record support and use the agreed access version.

Learner role

  • Exchange missing information before deciding and moving.
  • Take a meaningful turn, check the outcome and help partners clarify uncertainty.

Physical task and safety

5. Explain, check and improve

7 minutes · Report & repair · Pairs

Say this

Show your result. Explain one choice or one unclear part. Ask another group one useful question. Improve the result and show what you changed.

Procedure

  1. 0–2 min · Read one anonymous narrative aloud while its group shows the four scenes or hand-tableaux.
  2. 2–5 min · Peers identify the turning point and resolution, then ask one question about an unclear sequence.
  3. 5–7 min · Writers revise a sequence phrase or sentence link. Repeat one controlled transition if needed.

6. Independent evidence check

5 minutes · Exit check · Individual

Say this

Work on your own. Revise your story to 50–70 words. Mark the opening, ordered events, problem and ending. Show one comfortable two-count pose and a controlled change on the cue.

Procedure

  1. 0–1 min · Each learner independently revises the draft to 50–70 words, marking its opening, ordered events, turning point and resolution.
  2. 1–3 min · In pairs, show one agreed two-count pose and a controlled change on a spoken cue. Record support needs without judging acting ability.
  3. 3–5 min · Collect the writing and physical observations separately. Select one writing link and one movement transition for the next lesson.
