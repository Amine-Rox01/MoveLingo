Blank instruments and a proposed protocol · v7 · 27 October 2026

No pilot results are included. These forms help test usability, content suitability and consistency of observations. They cannot establish learning effectiveness by themselves.

1 · Proposed protocol

Invite three ELT experts and three PE experts to review the same 20 varied plans. Invite two teachers to pilot two supervised lessons each, subject to the school’s approval and appropriate consent. Keep learner names and sensitive details out of the app and research sheets.

  1. Record the app version, selection method, setting, dates and prior experience.
  2. Sample plans across levels, spaces, functions and adaptations. Review higher-risk cases explicitly.
  3. Experts independently rate relevance, safety suitability and clarity from 1 to 5, and explain required changes. Preserve disagreement; do not average away a safety concern.
  4. Teachers attempt defined app tasks before teaching. Record completion, errors, time, help needed and comments. Administer the standard SUS after use.
  5. Two observers independently rate the same agreed observation unit, such as the same coded learner in the same task and criterion. Agree the procedure in advance. Do not collect recordings without separate permission.
  6. Report missing data, limitations and changes. A four-lesson pilot is exploratory; it does not show causal learning gains.

Proposed decision rules from the evaluation report: aim for SUS ≥ 68 and unweighted Cohen’s κ ≥ 0.60, while examining specific failures and disagreement. These are local planning targets, not certification thresholds.

4 · Standard SUS response & scoring sheet

Administer the original ten-item System Usability Scale in its original order and wording, or a validated language version. Use Brooke’s published instrument: SUS instrument and instructions, hosted by AHRQ. The app’s short pilot survey is a separate local questionnaire and must not be reported as SUS.

Record answers below after administering that instrument: 1 = strongly disagree; 5 = strongly agree. The item text is not reproduced or paraphrased here, to preserve the standard instrument.

Scoring: subtract 1 from responses to odd-numbered items; subtract even-numbered responses from 5. Add all ten adjusted values and multiply by 2.5. The 0–100 result is a usability scale score, not a percentage. Do not calculate a score from incomplete responses in this sheet.

5 · Independent rubric agreement sheet

Enter two independent ratings of the same observation and criterion. Use the proposed local categories: 1 Emerging, 2 Developing, 3 Secure, 4 Transferable. “Not observed” is missing evidence; leave that pair blank. This tool excludes incomplete pairs and reports how many were excluded. It computes unweighted Cohen’s κ and raw agreement; small samples and uneven category use can make κ unstable.

7 · Sources and interpretation

  • Council of Europe (2020), CEFR Companion Volume: a reference framework; it does not validate MoveLingo’s activities.
  • British Council, A task-based approach: task, planning, reporting and responsive language work. MoveLingo offers a scaffolded, task-informed adaptation.
  • British Council, Total physical response: movement-based comprehension and suitable limits.
  • Coyle, Hood & Marsh (2010), CLIL, Cambridge University Press: integrating subject and language goals.
  • Brooke, J. (1996), “SUS: A ‘Quick and Dirty’ Usability Scale,” in Usability Evaluation in Industry, pp. 189–194. Original instrument and scoring.
  • Cohen, J. (1960), “A coefficient of agreement for nominal scales,” Educational and Psychological Measurement, 20(1), 37–46. κ = (observed agreement − expected agreement) / (1 − expected agreement).
